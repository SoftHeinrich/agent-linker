# TP / FN against SWATTR and ArTEMiS — where our recall comes from, and what it costs

Doc→model (SAD-SAM) on the 5 ArDoCo projects, 195 gold links. Reported arm
`s_linker110`, backend **terra** (GPT-5.6), **majority-of-3 runs**; ArTEMiS is
scored the same way on its own 3 GPT-5.6 runs; SWATTR is deterministic.
Everything below is regenerated by

```bash
python3 analysis/04_tpfn_vs_sota.py          # -> tpfn_vs_sota.txt, tpfn_vs_sota_goldlinks.csv
```

from committed link dumps (`model-doc/`), the ArDoCo benchmark (SAD text + PCM),
and the recorded run outputs in `results/` — no new LLM calls. Macro P/R/F1 of the
per-run sets reproduce `paper/table/rq1-results.csv` exactly
(ours .9140/.9669/.9385, ArTEMiS .8052/.8496/.8093, SWATTR .8695/.7717/.7990).

| **ours** | **186** | **24** | **9** | .886 | **.954** | **.919** |
| ArTEMiS | 157 | 44 | 38 | .781 | .805 | .793 |
| SWATTR | 148 | 40 | 47 | .787 | .759 | .773 |

Per project (TP/FP/FN):

| project | gold | ours | ArTEMiS | SWATTR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| mediastore | 31 | 31/3/**0** | 30/5/1 | 17/1/**14** |

This is not a precision/recall trade: we are ahead of both baselines on **both**

## 2. The gap is not spread over the gold — it is one stratum

Every gold link is classified by **how the sentence refers to the component** —
the same reference-form taxonomy the approach is built around (`approach.tex`
§ Approach: exact name / part of a name / coreference / a term the document coined):

| reference form | definition | N |
|---|---|---|
| **F1 exact-name** | the sentence writes the name (verbatim after case/separator folding) | 140 |
| **F2 partial-name** | the sentence writes some but not all words of a multi-word name | 29 |
| **F3 coreferent** | no word of the name in the sentence; the name occurs in the 3 preceding sentences | 19 |
| **F4 renamed** | no word of the name in the sentence *or* its 3-sentence context | 7 |

Recall by form:

| reference form | N | **ours** | ArTEMiS | SWATTR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| F1 exact-name | 140 | 136 (97.1 %) | 127 (90.7 %) | **139 (99.3 %)** |
| F2 partial-name | 29 | **25 (86.2 %)** | 12 (41.4 %) | 7 (24.1 %) |
| F3 coreferent | 19 | **18 (94.7 %)** | 12 (63.2 %) | 1 (5.3 %) |
| F4 renamed | 7 | **7 (100 %)** | 6 (85.7 %) | 1 (14.3 %) |
| **tail (F2–F4)** | **55** | **50 (90.9 %)** | 30 (54.5 %) | 9 (16.4 %) |
| ALL | 195 | 186 (95.4 %) | 157 (80.5 %) | 148 (75.9 %) |

Two things follow, and they are the whole story:

1. **On exact names, a 2018 string matcher is already at the ceiling — and beats
   us.** SWATTR recovers 139/140. There is nothing to win on F1, and we are in
   fact 3 links *behind* there (§5).
2. **28 % of the gold (55/195) is not an exact name, and that stratum is where
   every point of our advantage sits.** Decomposing the recall gap:

   | reference form | ours − SWATTR | ours − ArTEMiS |
   |---|---|---|
   | F1 exact-name | **−3** | +9 |
   | F2 partial-name | +18 | +13 |
   | F3 coreferent | +17 | +6 |
   | F4 renamed | +6 | +1 |
   | **total** | **+38** | **+29** |

   41 of the 42 links we recover and SWATTR does not are non-exact-name; 25 of
   the 36 ArTEMiS misses are.

## 3. Why each baseline loses that stratum — its FPs say it more clearly than its FNs

The two baselines fail the tail for *opposite* reasons, and each reason shows up
as a distinct false-positive signature.

| system | FP | anchored (name occurs in sentence) | unanchored (no name word) | code-identifier | drift (unanchored, same component was a TP ≤3 sentences earlier) | lexical over-firing (code-identifier **or** an all-lower-case use of a one-word name) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ours | **24** | 17 (71 %) | 7 (29 %) | 7 (29 %) | 2 (8 %) | 8 (33 %) |
| ArTEMiS | 44 | 16 (36 %) | **28 (64 %)** | 1 (2 %) | **17 (39 %)** | 2 (5 %) |
| SWATTR | 40 | **39 (98 %)** | 1 (2 %) | **27 (68 %)** | 1 (2 %) | **33 (82 %)** |

**SWATTR — a matcher with no discourse model.** 98 % of its FPs are anchored, and
**82 % are lexical over-firing**: 68 % fire on a *code identifier* rather than a reference, `logic.api`,
`storage.entity`, `common.datatransfer`, `e2e.cases`, `ui.website`, and the rest on
an ordinary-vocabulary use of a one-word name ("the core logic of the system").
32 of its 40 FPs are Teammates package-inventory sentences ("Package overview contains
logic.api, logic.core."). The same mechanism explains its FNs: a string matcher
fires exactly when the string is present, so it cannot reach "each client"
(F2: 7/29), cannot reach "It contains minimal logic beyond …" (F3: **1/19**),
and cannot reach a component the document renames (F4: 1/7 — it never links
MediaStore's `DB`, which the document only ever calls "the Database component",
or `FileStorage`, which it calls "a dedicated file server"). Those two components
receive **zero** links from SWATTR.

**ArTEMiS — recognition without per-sentence evidence.** Only 36 % of its FPs are
anchored; 39 % are *drift*: once its NER has recognised an entity it keeps
attaching it to a run of following sentences (TeaStore s29–s36 all `Recommender`,
s19–s21 all `Auth`, BBB s82–s84 all `Presentation Conversion`). The mirror of that
over-propagation is under-recognition: it loses 13 F2 links to us and, notably,
**13 exact-name links** that SWATTR gets for free — NER is a sampled recogniser,
so a name it does not tag is simply gone.

Two more axes where the difference is structural rather than incremental:

| | ours | ArTEMiS | SWATTR |
|---|---|---|---|
| components with **zero** recovered links | **0/40** | 1/40 (`GAE Datastore`) | 2/40 (`DB`, `FileStorage`) |
| recall on sentences with 2 gold targets | **96 %** | 65 % | 73 % |
| per-run TP spread over 3 runs | **1** (186/186/187) | 13 (152/148/161) | — (deterministic) |
| union − intersection over 3 runs | **7** | 26 | — |

ArTEMiS's collapse on 2-target sentences (65 % vs 85 % on 1-target sentences) is
the entity-recognition bottleneck showing itself: one mention region yields one
entity. Our three linkers propose independently and merge, so a sentence naming
two components is two independent cases.

## 4. Where our recall actually comes from — mechanism × reference form

The `source` column of every recovered link names the linker that proposed it.
Cross-tabulating that against the reference form shows the architecture doing
exactly what it was designed to do, with no mechanism carrying another's load:

| reference form | full-name linker | partial-name linker | coreference linker |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 exact-name (136) | **132** | 0 | 1 (+2 shared) |
| F2 partial-name (25) | 6 | **17** (+1 shared) | 1 |
| F3 coreferent (18) | 5 | 1 | **12** |
| F4 renamed (7) | 5 | 1 | 1 |

F4 is worth a sentence: those links are proposed by the *full-name* linker,
because the alias table has already turned "the Database component" into a full
name for `DB`. Alias discovery does not add a fourth linker; it moves a link that
is semantically distant into the cheapest, most precise mechanism we have.

Removing one design decision at a time (same 3 terra runs, majority-of-3) shows
which decision owns which stratum:

| arm | F1 exact | F2 partial | F3 coref | F4 renamed | ALL | FP |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Full (s110)** | 136/140 (97 %) | 25/29 (86 %) | 18/19 (95 %) | 7/7 (100 %) | **186/195** | **24** |
| − knowledge (no alias table) | 125 (89 %) | 21 (72 %) | 14 (74 %) | **2 (29 %)** | 162/195 | 17 |
| − decomposition (one LLM call) | 135 (96 %) | **9 (31 %)** | 13 (68 %) | 6 (86 %) | 163/195 | **47** |
| − evidence (judge without the bundle) | 135 (96 %) | 23 (79 %) | 17 (89 %) | 7 (100 %) | 182/195 | 25 |

- **The alias table owns F4.** Drop it and the renamed stratum falls 100 % → 29 %;
  it also costs 11 exact-name links, because a document-coined name *is* an exact
  name once the table knows it.
- **Form decomposition owns F2 and precision.** One monolithic call keeps exact
  names (96 %) but loses two thirds of the partial-name stratum (86 % → 31 %) and
  nearly doubles the false positives (24 → 47). This is the concrete content of
  "one rule strict enough for the last form is blind to the first": a single
  prompt cannot hold four standards of proof at once.
- **Evidence bundles are a recall-side safety rail here, not the main lever** —
  −4 TP at roughly unchanged FP at this operating point.

## 5. What it costs us: all 9 residual FNs, root-caused

Every one of our misses was traced to a specific verdict or alias-table entry in
the recorded logs. There are five root causes, not nine independent failures.

| # | link | form | root cause (verified in logs) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | BBB s19/s20/s21 `HTML5 Server` | F2 | **Wrong sibling in the alias table.** All 3 runs learn `bbb-html5 → HTML5 Client`; the gold assigns those sentences to `HTML5 Server`. The *same* wrong entry also produces 2 of our 24 FPs (`HTML5 Client` predicted at s20/s21). One alias entry ⇒ 5 of our 33 errors. |
| 4 | BBB s78 `BBB web` | F1 | **Alias sampling variance.** `bbb-web → BBB web` is discovered in run 2 only; runs 1 and 3 never propose the candidate. |
| 5 | BBB s26 `Apps` | F1 | **Joined-identifier rule.** The full-name judge returns `approve:false` in runs 1 and 3 for "…send events to **akka-apps**" — the prompt's rule that an expression occurring only inside a longer joined or dotted identifier names a piece of that identifier, not a participant. |
| 6–7 | TM s7, s8 `Logic` | F1 | **Generic-English-word rule.** Judge objection, verbatim: *"'the application back end logic' denotes logic/functionality, not the Logic component as an architectural participant."* |
| 8 | TM s168 `Test Driver` | F2 | **Coreference antecedent ambiguity.** *"'This component' has no unambiguous antecedent identifying Test Driver in the provided context."* (approved in run 3, rejected in 1 and 2.) |
| 9 | TS s26 `Persistence` | F3 | **Coreference antecedent ambiguity.** *"The antecedent is 'The persistence provider', which is not unambiguously the Persistence component."* (rejected in all 3 runs.) |

Causes 5–7 are not bugs; they are the *same two rules* that suppress SWATTR's
dominant error mode. SWATTR pays **33 of its 40 FPs** to lexical over-firing for having no such rules;
we pay 3 gold links for having them, and carry 8 such FPs instead of 33. On this
dataset the two rules cost 3 TP and avoid roughly 25 FP.

Cause 1 (sibling aliasing) is the one genuine defect, and it is the exact
phenomenon the partial-name scan's nesting refusal already guards against inside a
sentence — it just does not guard the alias table itself.

**These residuals are an operating point, not a blind spot.** The mirror backend
(`luna_s110`, identical pipeline) recovers **all 9** of them — 190/195 recall,
100 % on F1/F3/F4 — at FP 24 → 60. The pipeline can reach every one of these
links; terra's judges simply sit at a stricter point on the same curve.

## 6. Answering the two questions directly

**Where does our strength come from?** Not from being better at the task the
baselines already solve. On sentences that write the component's name, SWATTR
recovers 99.3 % and we recover 97.1 %. Our entire advantage — +38 links over
SWATTR, +29 over ArTEMiS — is recovered from the 28 % of the gold where the
sentence *does not* write the name: partial mentions (+18 / +13), coreference
(+17 / +6) and document-coined renamings (+6 / +1). Each of those strata is owned
by one named mechanism, and removing that mechanism collapses that stratum and no
other (§4).

**Which failure modes did we address?**

| failure mode | who suffers it | evidence | our answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cannot reach a partial mention ("each client") | SWATTR 7/29, ArTEMiS 12/29 | §2 | partial-name linker + target-blind denotation judge (17/25 of ours) |
| Cannot reach a coreferent mention ("It contains minimal logic …") | SWATTR **1/19** | §2 | coreference linker with a committed antecedent (12/18 of ours) |
| Vocabulary problem — the document renames the component | SWATTR 1/7 | §2; − knowledge arm 7→2 | confirmed alias table, scanned over every sentence |
| Over-firing on code identifiers (`logic.api`, `storage.entity`) | SWATTR 27/40 FPs | §3 | joined/dotted-identifier rule in the judge (costs us 1 TP) |
| Over-firing on ordinary English words that happen to be names | SWATTR 33/40 FPs are lexical over-firing vs our 8/24 | §3 | capitalisation-aware generic-word rule (costs us 2 TP) |
| Topic drift — an entity propagated over a run of sentences | ArTEMiS 17/44 FPs | §3 | every candidate carries evidence matched **in its own sentence**; ours 2/24 |
| One entity per mention region | ArTEMiS 65 % on 2-target sentences | §3 | three linkers propose independently, merge by pair |
| Whole components silently unlinked | SWATTR 2/40, ArTEMiS 1/40 | §3 | 0/40 |
| Run-to-run instability | ArTEMiS spread 13 TP per run, 26 union−∩ | §1 | spread 1 per run, 7 union−∩ |
