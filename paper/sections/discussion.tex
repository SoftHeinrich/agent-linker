\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}

\paragraph{Internal validity.}
Three factors could bias our measurements.
First, the \ac{LLM} backend is non-deterministic.
We report our system as the mean of three runs and release every raw response~\cite{replication}.

Second, the backend may have seen the five public projects during pre-training, so memorization rather than the workflow could explain the gain.
The strongest baseline \Artemis{} runs on the same backend and shares this exposure, so the comparison stays fair.
% Third, the gain could come from the extra \ac{LLM} calls rather than the workflow itself; the ablations (\ref{rq:validators}, \ref{rq:ablation}) rule this out by attributing it to specific modules, each contributing a non-redundant slice of links.

\paragraph{External validity.}
We measure on the five Java systems of the ARDoCo benchmark, the largest curated one we know of~\cite{fuchs_establishing_2023}; results may not transfer to other languages, industrial systems, or differently written documents.
We mitigate this by using all five projects, which span two orders of magnitude in size and several domains, and by keeping \approach{} training-free with no benchmark-specific tuning.
To show the gains do not depend on one model, we run \approach{} on two backends, GPT-5.4 and Claude Sonnet.

\paragraph{Construct validity.}
\ref{rq:metrics} assumes that recovering every documented component matters to developers; our size-aware metrics express this through sentence coverage on both tasks, worst-component and harmonic-mean per-component \fone{} on doc-code, and the silent-failure mass (SFM) on doc-model, the share of documented sentences whose component a system abandons.
These are proxies, so we keep the standard link-level \fone{} as the reference and report the size-aware view beside it.
They are also not a relabeling of \fone{}: a system can score well on \fone{} and still leave whole documented components untraced, which is exactly what the size-aware view exposes.
