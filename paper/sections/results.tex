This is because \approach{} builds the alias table once and reuses it across every sentence, then filters each link through the judge stack, so it proposes a link wherever a name or a reference occurs instead of only where a single extraction pass fires.
\Artemis{} extracts entities with no reusable architecture knowledge and no judging, so it neither reaches the alias-heavy mentions nor removes the plausible-but-wrong links an \ac{LLM} produces.

The gain is not a precision-for-recall trade.
On doc-model \approach{} leads on both components of the score, precision $0.93$ against $0.81$ ($+12.6$pp) and recall $0.98$ against $0.85$ ($+12.6$pp); on doc-code it leads by $+13.5$pp precision and $+3.9$pp recall.
The scan-plus-judge split is what makes both possible at once: the scans over-propose by design, marking every sentence that writes a name or a word of one rather than extracting one mention, and the judges remove what they over-propose before the link set is emitted.
A single extraction pass has to make that trade in one decision and cannot recover a mention it did not emit.

\autoref{tab:rq3-confusion} reports, for each judge, the links it rejects and keeps and what the pipeline loses when it is switched off, averaged over the three runs.
The \nameValidator{} rejects $95.0$ false positives while outright costing $5.0$ true ones; the \corefValidator{} rejects $57.3$ against $2.7$.
The REJ-TP column counts only the true links no other linker recovers, so it is the recall each judge costs the pipeline outright, and the two numbers behind it are far apart: the \corefValidator{} rejects $49.0$ true links a run and only $2.7$ of them are lost, because \linkerN{} has already proposed the rest.
Together the two judges reject $146.0$ distinct false positives and lose $6.0$ true links; the per-judge counts overlap, so they do not sum to these totals.
This is because each judge reads a different kind of case: the \nameValidator{} reads a match the code computed, with the evidence that match carries, and the \corefValidator{} reads a resolution the model committed to, with the antecedent it quoted.

The judges are what makes the scan usable, and \ftwo\ prices how much of that is precision.
Switching both off drops macro \fone\ from $0.952$ to $0.802$, a loss of $14.9$pp, but macro \ftwo\ only from $0.965$ to $0.900$, a loss of $6.5$pp.
Removing them one at a time costs $9.9$ and $7.8$pp of \fone\ (\nameValidator{}, \corefValidator{}) against $4.2$ and $2.8$pp of \ftwo.
This is because a judge almost only removes links: it buys precision and can never add recall, so a metric that weights recall four times as heavily as precision sees a third of the effect.
The \corefValidator{} carries the most weight per case, because it judges the least precise input: of the $144.0$ candidates it sees only $37.7$ survive, against $194.7$ of $295.0$ at the \nameValidator{}.
The price is judging volume: the stack rules on $439.0$ candidates per run to emit $211.3$ links, roughly two decisions per link kept.
Therefore the judge stack, not the linker, is what turns a high-recall scan into a precise result---and a reader who cares mostly about recall should read its contribution as the smaller \ftwo\ figure.

\begin{rqanswer}
\paragraph{Answer to RQ3.}
The two judges together reject $146.0$ distinct false positives while outright costing $6.0$ true links; removing both costs $14.9$pp macro \fone\ but only $6.5$pp macro \ftwo.
They read different kinds of case---a match the code computed, a resolution the model committed to---and buy precision, never recall.
\end{rqanswer}

\approach{} reaches three reference forms through two linkers over one knowledge module, so it is ablated by form: the \linkerB{} and the \linkerD{} inside \linkerN{}, the \linkerC{}, and the alias table all three read.
Every link records the proposer that reached it, so the two forms \linkerN{} judges together can still be separated after the fact.
\autoref{tab:rq4} reports the five variants on doc-code, with the metrics ordered from the standard link-level \fone\ to the tail.

The \linkerB{} alone reaches an \avgfone\ of $0.881$, already above SWATTR's $0.799$: an \ac{LLM} base beats the lexical pipeline on its own.
Adding the \linkerD{} and the \linkerC{} raises \avgfone\ to $0.952$, because they reach the mentions the \linkerB{} cannot quote in full.
The two smaller forms are worth half again as much under \ftwo\ as under \fone: \avgftwo\ rises from $0.850$ to $0.965$ ($+11.6$pp) where \avgfone\ rises $7.1$pp, because what they add is recall.
The three forms catch different slices of gold links---the \linkerB{} contributes $137$ unique true positives, the \linkerD{} another $16$, and the \linkerC{} another $15$---so the decomposition is not redundant, and it survives the two written forms being judged by one rule.

The table tells the RQ2 story from inside the approach.
Removing the \linkerD{} or the \linkerC{} barely moves the standard link-level \fone---$-2.0$pp and $-4.5$pp---but each one lowers the tail: with the \linkerB{} alone, worst-component \fone\ falls from $0.82$ to $0.73$ and the harmonic per-component \fone\ from $0.94$ to $0.87$.
Removing the \linkerB{} is different---it collapses every metric, link-level \fone\ included, by $58.9$pp, because it recovers most of the direct links.
Read as whole systems the two smaller forms score far lower---the \linkerD{} $0.085$ \avgfone\ and the \linkerC{} $0.311$---but that is their scope, not their quality: each fires only on the sentences its evidence form applies to (the \linkerD{} skips any sentence that writes a whole name, the \linkerC{} only sentences carrying a referring expression), so a row scored against the whole gold standard charges it for every sentence it was never meant to see.

The knowledge module is ablated as the no-knowledge variant of \ref{rq:ablation}: the document-alias stage is switched off and every scan sees canonical component names only.
Removing the alias table costs $6.1$pp \avgfone\ ($0.952 \rightarrow 0.890$) and $10.6$pp \avgftwo\ ($0.965 \rightarrow 0.860$) on doc-model, a contribution no linker recovers on its own.
Its real weight is on the tail, exactly where \autoref{tab:rq2} says the standard metric is blind: worst-component \fone\ falls from $0.82$ to $0.47$ ($-34.8$pp) and the harmonic per-component \fone\ from $0.94$ to $0.55$ ($-39.1$pp), against $-11.4$pp of doc-code file \fone.
This is because an alias is the only route to a component the document never names canonically, and those components are small: losing them costs little link volume and a whole architectural unit.
Therefore the modules are complementary rather than interchangeable, and the knowledge module and the two cheap forms all buy tail accuracy that the standard metric does not price.

\begin{rqanswer}
\paragraph{Answer to RQ4.}
The \linkerB{} alone beats SWATTR; the \linkerD{} and the \linkerC{} lift \avgfone\ from $0.881$ to $0.952$ and \avgftwo\ from $0.850$ to $0.965$; the knowledge module is worth $6.1$pp \avgfone\ and $10.6$pp \avgftwo.
Removing a small form or the knowledge module barely moves link-level \fone\ but lowers the tail---the alias table alone is worth $34.8$pp of worst-component \fone---while removing the \linkerB{} collapses every metric.
\end{rqanswer}

\subsection{Summary}
\label{sec:results:summary}

\approach{} improves over the strongest baseline, \Artemis{}, run on the same backend, at every granularity we measure.
Doc-model \avgfone\ rises from $0.809$ to $0.952$ ($+14.2$pp) and \avgftwo\ from $0.828$ to $0.965$ ($+13.7$pp), with all five projects gaining on both.
The doc-code composition inherits the gain: $+10.6$pp \avgfone\ and $+7.1$pp \avgftwo.
The \cmrname{} is $0.0\%$, against $3.3\%$ for \Artemis{} and $7.1\%$ for SWATTR.
The doc-code size-aware metrics show a much larger advantage on the tail that the standard metric hides: worst-component \fone\ $+35.3$pp and harmonic-mean per-component \fone\ $+30.6$pp over \Artemis{}, against $+10.6$pp at the link level.
The two judges remove $146.0$ distinct false positives at a cost of $6.0$ true links---worth $14.9$pp of \fone\ but only $6.5$pp of \ftwo, since a judge buys precision and never recall---and each of the three reference forms catches a non-overlapping slice of true positives.
The knowledge module is worth $6.1$pp \avgfone, and $34.8$pp of worst-component \fone: like the size-aware suite itself, its value is concentrated in the tail.
We discuss the threats to validity in~\autoref{sec:threats}.
