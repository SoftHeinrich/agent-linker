\label{sec:results:rq1}

\autoref{tab:rq1} reports macro precision, recall, \fone, and \ftwo\ for every system on both tasks, doc-model and doc-code.
Both \ac{LLM} systems run on the same backend and are reported as the mean of three runs, so the comparison isolates the workflow from the model.
On doc-model, \approach{} lifts the \avgfone\ from \Artemis{}'s $0.809$ to $0.914$, a gain of $+10.4$pp; the lexical SWATTR pipeline reaches $0.799$.
The recall-weighted \avgftwo\ moves by the same margin: $0.828 \rightarrow 0.931$ ($+10.3$pp).
This is because \approach{} builds the alias table once and reuses it across every sentence, then filters each link through the judge stack, so it proposes a link wherever a name or a reference occurs instead of only where a single extraction pass fires.
\Artemis{} extracts entities with no reusable architecture knowledge and no judging, so it neither reaches the alias-heavy mentions nor removes the plausible-but-wrong links an \ac{LLM} produces.

The gain is not a precision-for-recall trade.
On doc-model \approach{} leads on both components of the score, precision $0.89$ against $0.81$ ($+8.2$pp) and recall $0.94$ against $0.85$ ($+9.4$pp); on doc-code it leads by $+11.2$pp precision and $+4.1$pp recall.
The scan-plus-judge split is what makes both possible at once: the entity pass over-proposes by design, scanning each sentence for every candidate name rather than extracting one mention, and the judges remove what it over-proposes before the link set is emitted.
A single extraction pass has to make that trade in one decision and cannot recover a mention it did not emit.

Beyond the link-level average, \autoref{tab:rq2} reports the \cmrname{} (\cmr), the doc-model size-aware metric of \autoref{sec:metric:suite}: the share of gold sentence--component assignments whose component a system abandons.
\approach{} reaches a macro \cmr{} of $1.9\%$, against $3.3\%$ for \Artemis{} and $7.1\%$ for the lexical SWATTR pipeline.
It abandons one documented component, MediaStore's \texttt{FileStorage}, which carries $3$ of that project's $31$ documented sentence--component assignments; the same component in all three runs, and the other four projects have a \cmr{} of $0.0\%$.
\Artemis{} abandons two components per run on average, and only one of them consistently: Teammates' \texttt{GAE Datastore} in every run, plus Teammates' \texttt{Common}, BigBlueButton's \texttt{Presentation Conversion}, or JabRef's \texttt{preferences} depending on the run.
Which components a single \Artemis{} run drops is therefore not stable, which is itself the finding---a re-run repairs one hole and opens another.
SWATTR abandons three MediaStore components---\texttt{DB}, \texttt{FileStorage}, and \texttt{Reencoding}---that together carry $35.5\%$ of MediaStore's documented sentence--component assignments.

\paragraph{Smallest-gain case: MediaStore.}
%SPEC [t8] The section's one honest-failure paragraph (Wang T8): own the single worst
%  datum, name the cause, close on the aggregate. The datum moved from Teammates
%  (component \fone) to MediaStore.
%DONE (2026-08-29) against the matched-backend baseline MediaStore is no longer only a
%  doc-model \fone\ regression -- it loses on doc-model \fone, doc-model \ftwo\ and
%  doc-code \fone\ alike, so the paragraph states all three instead of leaning on \ftwo.
MediaStore is the one project where \approach{} does not beat \Artemis{}, and it loses on every view of the score: doc-model \fone\ $0.903$ against $0.933$ ($-3.0$pp), doc-model \ftwo\ $0.903$ against $0.954$ ($-5.0$pp), and doc-code \fone\ $0.867$ against $0.927$ ($-6.1$pp).
It is also the project carrying the arm's only missed component, and the two facts share one cause.
All three of \texttt{FileStorage}'s documented sentences call the component ``the DataStorage'', a name the architecture model does not use, so neither name pass matches it and the alias table never learns the pair.
\linkerC{} does propose all three links, and the \corefValidator{} rejects all three on the grounds that an explicitly named mention is not a coreference---correct by its own rule, and wrong for this component.
On MediaStore the judges therefore reject $8$ true links and $0$ false ones, the mirror image of their aggregate behaviour.
MediaStore is also the smallest and least enrolled project in the benchmark, so a handful of links moves its score further than anywhere else; the other four projects gain between $+6.5$pp (Teammates) and $+27.9$pp (TeaStore) of doc-model \fone.
The fix is an alias source that covers names the model does not contain, which the current knowledge module derives only from model element names.

The same picture holds on the downstream doc-code task, where \approach{} reaches code files transitively.
\approach{} lifts the doc-code \avgfone\ from \Artemis{}'s $0.813$ to $0.909$ ($+9.6$pp) and the \avgftwo\ from $0.844$ to $0.912$ ($+6.8$pp); the heuristic \TransArc{} pipeline reaches $0.803$ and $0.782$.
This is because the composition step inherits the doc-model gain at every sentence, so the advantage \approach{} earns over \Artemis{} upstream carries through to the task developers care about.

\begin{rqanswer}
\paragraph{Answer to RQ1.}
Against \Artemis{} on the same backend, \approach{} gains $+10.4$pp \avgfone\ and $+10.3$pp \avgftwo\ on doc-model, and $+9.6$pp \avgfone\ and $+6.8$pp \avgftwo\ on doc-code.
It leads on precision and recall at both grains, and abandons one component to \Artemis{}'s two and SWATTR's three.
\end{rqanswer}

\autoref{tab:rq2} reports the size-aware suite for \approach{}, \TransArc{}, and \Artemis{} on both tasks, on the GPT-5.6-terra backend.
On doc-model, the \cmrname{} reported above already separates the systems: $1.9\%$ for \approach{} against $3.3\%$ and $7.1\%$.

On doc-code, the standard link-level \fone\ shows the \emph{smaller} gap: \approach{} separates from \Artemis{} by $+9.6$pp, because it is driven by the few large components every system recovers.
The size-aware metrics open it.
\approach{} reaches a worst-component \fone\ of $0.73$ against \Artemis{}'s $0.47$ ($+25.8$pp, roughly $2.7$ times the link-level \fone\ gap), and a harmonic per-component \fone\ of $0.90$ against $0.63$ ($+26.4$pp).
Against the deterministic \TransArc{} pipeline the same pattern holds with a smaller spread: $+10.6$pp link-level, $+21.2$pp worst-component, $+22.3$pp harmonic.

Averaging over runs understates how far \Artemis{} drops.
On three of five projects---Teammates, BigBlueButton, and JabRef---at least one \Artemis{} run scores \fone\ $0$ on a gold component, missing it entirely; a different project each run, which is why no single project's mean falls to zero.
\approach{} never drops a doc-code component to zero on any project in any run; its lowest per-component \fone\ is $0.40$, on Teammates.
This is the failure mode link-level \fone\ cannot show: a missed small component costs almost nothing in a volume-weighted average, but a developer who reads the architecture still sees the hole.
The gap is largest against the \ac{LLM} baseline \Artemis{}, whose per-pass extraction drops whole components and drops different ones on re-run; it narrows against \TransArc{}, which is more stable on the tail but trails on the link level.
Against either baseline the size-aware metrics, not the standard metric, show where the systems differ.

\begin{rqanswer}
\paragraph{Answer to RQ2.}
On doc-model, \approach{} reaches a $1.9\%$ \cmrname{}, below both baselines'.
On doc-code the standard \fone\ gap is $+9.6$pp; the size-aware suite opens it to $+25.8$pp worst-component and $+26.4$pp harmonic-mean \fone, exposing the tail the baselines drop.
\end{rqanswer}

\subsection{Answer to RQ3}
\label{sec:results:rq3}

\input{table/rq3-confusion}
%SPEC [rq3] tab:rq3-confusion = one row per judge: the links it rejects and keeps, plus the
%  \fone/\ftwo the pipeline loses with that judge switched off. Terminology = TP/FP throughout.
%DONE [3-judge] this arm judges three linkers, not two: the named-mention stage is split into
%  a full-name and a partial-name pass, each with its own judge. Mean of 3 runs
%  (mini-rq34/reports_s92a/rq3_validators.csv + rq3_variants.csv).
%DONE [f2] the table now carries the counterfactual in BOTH \fone and \ftwo, which is what
%  separates the judges' precision effect from their net effect (see the second paragraph).
\autoref{tab:rq3-confusion} reports, for each judge, the links it rejects and keeps and what the pipeline loses when it is switched off, averaged over the three runs.
The \entValidator{} rejects $39.7$ false positives while outright costing $3.3$ true ones; the \partValidator{} rejects $58.0$ against $7.0$; the \corefValidator{} rejects $72.0$ against $9.3$.
The REJ-TP column counts only the true links no other linker recovers, so it is the recall each judge costs the pipeline outright.
Together the three judges reject $164.3$ distinct false positives and lose $14.0$ true links; the per-judge counts overlap, so they do not sum to these totals.
This is because each judge targets a different failure mode: a full name that matches no model element, a partial name that denotes something else, and an implicit reference whose antecedent the linker could not quote.

The judges are what makes the scan usable, and \ftwo\ prices how much of that is precision.
Switching all three off drops macro \fone\ from $0.914$ to $0.769$, a loss of $14.5$pp, but macro \ftwo\ only from $0.931$ to $0.882$, a loss of $4.9$pp.
Removing them one at a time costs $4.0$, $5.1$, and $8.6$pp of \fone\ (\entValidator{}, \partValidator{}, \corefValidator{}) against $1.4$, $1.3$, and $1.9$pp of \ftwo.
This is because a judge almost only removes links: it buys precision and can never add recall, so a metric that weights recall four times as heavily as precision sees a third of the effect.
The \corefValidator{} carries the most weight either way, because it judges the least precise input: only $31.7$ of the $187.7$ candidates it sees survive, and $74.7$ of them are false.
The price is judging volume: the stack rules on $491.3$ candidates per run to emit $213.3$ links, roughly two decisions per link kept.
Therefore the judge stack, not the linker, is what turns a high-recall scan into a precise result---and a reader who cares mostly about recall should read its contribution as the smaller \ftwo\ figure.

\begin{rqanswer}
\paragraph{Answer to RQ3.}
The three judges together reject $164.3$ distinct false positives while outright costing $14.0$ true links; removing all three costs $14.5$pp macro \fone\ but only $4.9$pp macro \ftwo.
They target separate failure modes---an unmatched full name, a misdenoting partial name, an unquotable implicit reference---and buy precision, never recall.
\end{rqanswer}

\approach{} has four modules: the knowledge module and the three linker modules \linkerB{}, \linkerD{}, and \linkerC{}.
We ablate each one and score the result with the size-aware suite.
\autoref{tab:rq4} reports the five variants on doc-code, with the metrics ordered from the standard link-level \fone\ to the tail.

\linkerB{} alone reaches an \avgfone\ of $0.856$, already above SWATTR's $0.799$: an \ac{LLM} base beats the lexical pipeline on its own.
Adding \linkerD{} and \linkerC{} raises \avgfone\ to $0.914$, because they reach the mentions \linkerB{} cannot quote in full.
The two smaller linkers are worth twice as much under \ftwo\ as under \fone: \avgftwo\ rises from $0.818$ to $0.931$ ($+11.3$pp) where \avgfone\ rises $5.8$pp, because what they add is recall.
The three linkers catch different slices of gold links---\linkerB{} contributes $137$ unique true positives, \linkerD{} another $15$, and \linkerC{} another $17$---so the decomposition is not redundant.

The table tells the RQ2 story from inside the approach.
Removing \linkerD{} or \linkerC{} barely moves the standard link-level \fone---$-0.4$pp and $-5.0$pp---but each one lowers the tail: with \linkerB{} alone, worst-component \fone\ falls from $0.73$ to $0.65$ and the harmonic per-component \fone\ from $0.90$ to $0.85$.
Removing \linkerB{} is different---it collapses every metric, link-level \fone\ included, by $60.0$pp, because \linkerB{} recovers most of the direct links.
Read as whole systems the two smaller linkers score far lower---\linkerD{} $0.078$ \avgfone\ and \linkerC{} $0.261$---but that is their scope, not their quality: each fires only on the sentences its evidence form applies to (\linkerD{} skips any sentence that states a whole name, \linkerC{} only sentences carrying a referring expression), so a row scored against the whole gold standard charges it for every sentence it was never meant to see.

The knowledge module is ablated as the no-knowledge variant of \ref{rq:ablation}: the document-alias stage is switched off and every linker sees canonical component names only.
Removing the alias table costs $6.2$pp \avgfone\ ($0.914 \rightarrow 0.851$) and $9.6$pp \avgftwo\ ($0.931 \rightarrow 0.835$) on doc-model, a contribution no linker recovers on its own.
Its real weight is on the tail, exactly where \autoref{tab:rq2} says the standard metric is blind: worst-component \fone\ falls from $0.73$ to $0.42$ ($-30$pp) and the harmonic per-component \fone\ from $0.90$ to $0.64$ ($-26$pp), against $-9.6$pp of doc-code file \fone.
This is because an alias is the only route to a component the document never names canonically, and those components are small: losing them costs little link volume and a whole architectural unit.
Therefore the modules are complementary rather than interchangeable, and the knowledge module and the two cheap linkers all buy tail accuracy that the standard metric does not price.

\begin{rqanswer}
\paragraph{Answer to RQ4.}
\linkerB{} alone beats SWATTR; \linkerD{} and \linkerC{} lift \avgfone\ from $0.856$ to $0.914$ and \avgftwo\ from $0.818$ to $0.931$; the knowledge module is worth $6.2$pp \avgfone\ and $9.6$pp \avgftwo.
Removing a small linker or the knowledge module barely moves link-level \fone\ but lowers the tail---the alias table alone is worth $30$pp of worst-component \fone---while removing \linkerB{} collapses every metric.
\end{rqanswer}

\subsection{Summary}
\label{sec:results:summary}

\approach{} improves over the strongest baseline, \Artemis{}, run on the same backend, at every granularity we measure.
Doc-model \avgfone\ rises from $0.809$ to $0.914$ ($+10.4$pp) and \avgftwo\ from $0.828$ to $0.931$ ($+10.3$pp), with four of five projects gaining on both.
The doc-code composition inherits the gain: $+9.6$pp \avgfone\ and $+6.8$pp \avgftwo.
The \cmrname{} is $1.9\%$, against $3.3\%$ for \Artemis{} and $7.1\%$ for SWATTR.
The doc-code size-aware metrics show a much larger advantage on the tail that the standard metric hides: worst-component \fone\ $+25.8$pp and harmonic-mean per-component \fone\ $+26.4$pp over \Artemis{}, against $+9.6$pp at the link level.
The three judges remove $164.3$ distinct false positives at a cost of $14.0$ true links---worth $14.5$pp of \fone\ but only $4.9$pp of \ftwo, since a judge buys precision and never recall---and each of the three linkers catches a non-overlapping slice of true positives.
The knowledge module is worth $6.2$pp \avgfone, and $30$pp of worst-component \fone: like the size-aware suite itself, its value is concentrated in the tail.
We discuss the threats to validity in~\autoref{sec:threats}.
