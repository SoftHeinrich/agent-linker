\subsection{Answer to RQ1}
\label{sec:results:rq1}

\autoref{tab:rq1} reports macro precision, recall, \fone, and \ftwo\ for every system on both tasks, doc-model and doc-code.
On doc-model, \approach{} lifts the \avgfone\ from \Artemis{}'s $0.836$ to $0.914$, a gain of $+7.8$pp; the lexical SWATTR pipeline reaches $0.799$.
The gain is larger under the recall-weighted \avgftwo: $0.790 \rightarrow 0.931$ ($+14.1$pp).
This is because \approach{} builds the alias table once and reuses it across every sentence, then filters each link through the judge stack, so it proposes a link wherever a name or a reference occurs instead of only where a single extraction pass fires.
\Artemis{} extracts entities with no reusable architecture knowledge and no judging, so it neither reaches the alias-heavy mentions nor removes the plausible-but-wrong links an \ac{LLM} produces.

The two systems miss in opposite directions.
\approach{} recovers $0.94$ of the gold links against \Artemis{}'s $0.76$ ($+18.1$pp recall), and pays for it with precision: $0.89$ against \Artemis{}'s $0.93$.
Its noise rate is $10.4\%$, above \Artemis{}'s $7.2\%$ and below the lexical pipeline's $13.7\%$.
This is the trade the entity pass makes deliberately: it scans the sentence for every candidate name rather than extracting one mention, and the judges then reject what the scan over-proposes.
Therefore \approach{} leads on both balanced and recall-weighted \fone, and \Artemis{} keeps the cleaner output.

Beyond the link-level average, \autoref{tab:rq2} reports the silent-failure mass (SFM), the doc-model size-aware metric of \autoref{sec:metric:suite}: the share of documented sentences whose component a system abandons.
\approach{} reaches a macro SFM of $2.2\%$, against $7.1\%$ for \Artemis{} and $7.4\%$ for the lexical SWATTR pipeline.
It abandons one documented component on one project, MediaStore's \texttt{FileStorage}, which carries $3$ of that project's $27$ documented sentences; the other four projects have an SFM of $0.0\%$.
\Artemis{} abandons one component on three of five projects: \texttt{GAE Datastore} on Teammates, \texttt{Presentation Conversion} on BigBlueButton, and \texttt{preferences} on JabRef.
SWATTR abandons three MediaStore components---\texttt{DB}, \texttt{FileStorage}, and \texttt{Reencoding}---that together carry $37.0\%$ of MediaStore's documented sentences.

\paragraph{Smallest-gain case: MediaStore.}
%SPEC [t8] The section's one honest-failure paragraph (Wang T8): own the single worst
%  datum, name the cause, close on the aggregate. The datum moved from Teammates
%  (component \fone) to MediaStore (the only doc-model \fone\ regression on this arm).
MediaStore is the one project where \approach{} does not beat \Artemis{} on doc-model \fone: $0.903$ against $0.931$, a regression of $-2.8$pp.
It is also the project carrying the arm's only silent failure, and the two facts share one cause.
All three of \texttt{FileStorage}'s documented sentences call the component ``the DataStorage'', a name the architecture model does not use, so neither name pass matches it and the alias table never learns the pair.
\linkerC{} does propose all three links, and the \corefValidator{} rejects all three on the grounds that an explicitly named mention is not a coreference---correct by its own rule, and wrong for this component.
On MediaStore the judges therefore reject $8$ true links and $0$ false ones, the mirror image of their aggregate behaviour.
The regression does not survive the recall-weighted view---MediaStore gains $+0.9$pp \ftwo---and the other four projects gain between $+3.2$pp (JabRef) and $+16.7$pp (TeaStore) of \fone.
The fix is an alias source that covers names the model does not contain, which the current knowledge module derives only from model element names.

The same picture holds on the downstream doc-code task, where \approach{} reaches code files transitively.
\approach{} lifts the doc-code \avgfone\ from \Artemis{}'s $0.849$ to $0.909$ ($+6.0$pp) and the \avgftwo\ from $0.804$ to $0.912$ ($+10.8$pp); the heuristic \TransArc{} pipeline reaches $0.803$ and $0.782$.
This is because the composition step inherits the doc-model gain at every sentence, so the advantage \approach{} earns over \Artemis{} upstream carries through to the task developers care about.

\begin{rqanswer}
\paragraph{Answer to RQ1.}
\approach{} improves over \Artemis{}, the strongest baseline, by $+7.8$pp \avgfone\ and $+14.1$pp \avgftwo\ on doc-model, and $+6.0$pp \avgfone\ and $+10.8$pp \avgftwo\ on doc-code.
It trades $4$pp of doc-model precision for $18$pp of recall, and abandons one documented component where each baseline abandons three.
\end{rqanswer}

\autoref{tab:rq2} reports the size-aware suite for \approach{}, \TransArc{}, and \Artemis{} on both tasks, on the GPT-5.6-terra backend.
On doc-model, \approach{} covers $0.96$ of the documented sentences, against $0.79$ for both baselines ($+17$pp).
Combined with the $2.2\%$ silent-failure mass reported above, \approach{} reaches every documented component but one and nearly every documented sentence.

On doc-code, the standard link-level \fone\ shows the \emph{smallest} gap: \approach{} separates from \Artemis{} by $+6.0$pp, because it is driven by the few large components every system recovers.
The size-aware metrics open the gap.
\approach{} reaches a sentence coverage of $0.88$ against \Artemis{}'s $0.71$ ($+17$pp), because the judges reject links whose anchor is absent from the document while the linkers collectively visit every sentence at least once.

The difference is largest on the tail metrics.
\approach{} reaches a worst-component \fone\ of $0.71$ against \Artemis{}'s $0.35$ ($+36$pp, six times the link-level \fone\ gap).

\approach{} never drops a doc-code component to zero; its lowest per-component \fone\ is $0.33$, on Teammates.
The harmonic mean of the per-component \fone\ tells the same story without relying on a single component: $0.87$ for \approach{} against \Artemis{}'s $0.47$ ($+41$pp).
This is the failure mode link-level \fone\ cannot show: a missed small component costs almost nothing in a volume-weighted average, but a developer who reads the architecture still sees the hole.
The gap is largest against the \ac{LLM} baseline \Artemis{}, whose per-pass extraction drops whole components; it narrows against the deterministic \TransArc{} pipeline, which is more stable on the tail but trails on coverage.
Against either baseline the size-aware metrics, not the standard metric, show where the systems differ.

\begin{rqanswer}
\paragraph{Answer to RQ2.}
On doc-model, \approach{} covers $0.96$ of sentences ($+17$pp) at $2.2\%$ silent-failure mass, a third of either baseline's.
On doc-code the standard \fone\ gap is smallest ($+6.0$pp); the size-aware suite opens it---coverage $+17$pp, worst-component \fone\ $+36$pp, harmonic-mean \fone\ $+41$pp---exposing the tail the baselines drop.
\end{rqanswer}

%DONE [3-judge] this arm judges three linkers, not two: the named-mention stage is split into

\autoref{tab:rq3-confusion} reports, for each judge, the links it rejects and keeps and what the pipeline loses when it is switched off, averaged over the three runs.
The \entValidator{} rejects $39.7$ false positives while outright costing $3.3$ true ones; the \partValidator{} rejects $58.0$ against $7.0$; the \corefValidator{} rejects $72.0$ against $9.3$.
The REJ-TP column counts only the true links no other linker recovers, so it is the recall each judge costs the pipeline outright.
Together the three judges reject $164.3$ distinct false positives and lose $14.0$ true links; the per-judge counts overlap, so they do not sum to these totals.
This is because each judge targets a different failure mode: a full name that matches no model element, a partial name that denotes something else, and an implicit reference whose antecedent the linker could not quote.

The judges are what makes the scan usable, and \ftwo\ prices how much of that is precision.
Switching all three off drops macro \fone\ from $0.914$ to $0.769$, a loss of $14.5$pp, but macro \ftwo\ only from $0.931$ to $0.882$, a loss of $4.9$pp.
Removing them one at a time costs $4.0$, $5.1$, and $8.6$pp of \fone\ (\entValidator{}, \partValidator{}, \corefValidator{}) against $1.4$, $1.3$, and $1.9$pp of \ftwo.
This is because a judge almost only removes links: it buys precision and can never add recall, so a metric that weights recall four times as heavily as precision sees a third of the effect.
The \corefValidator{} carries the most weight either way, because it judges the least precise input: only $31.7$ of the $187.7$ candidates it sees survive, and $74.7$ of them are false.
The price is judging volume: the stack rules on $491.3$ candidates per run to emit $213.3$ links, roughly two decisions per link kept.
Therefore the judge stack, not the linker, is what turns a high-recall scan into a precise result---and a reader who cares mostly about recall should read its contribution as the smaller \ftwo\ figure.

\begin{rqanswer}
\paragraph{Answer to RQ3.}
The three judges together reject $164.3$ distinct false positives while outright costing $14.0$ true links; removing all three costs $14.5$pp macro \fone\ but only $4.9$pp macro \ftwo.
They target separate failure modes---an unmatched full name, a misdenoting partial name, an unquotable implicit reference---and buy precision, never recall.
\end{rqanswer}

\approach{} has four modules: the knowledge module and the three linker modules \linkerB{}, \linkerD{}, and \linkerC{}.
We ablate each one and score the result with the size-aware suite.
\autoref{tab:rq4} reports the five variants on doc-code, with the metrics ordered from the standard link-level \fone\ to the tail.

\linkerB{} alone reaches an \avgfone\ of $0.856$, already above SWATTR's $0.799$: an \ac{LLM} base beats the lexical pipeline on its own.
Adding \linkerD{} and \linkerC{} raises \avgfone\ to $0.914$, because they reach the mentions \linkerB{} cannot quote in full.
The two smaller linkers are worth twice as much under \ftwo\ as under \fone: \avgftwo\ rises from $0.818$ to $0.931$ ($+11.3$pp) where \avgfone\ rises $5.8$pp, because what they add is recall.
The three linkers catch different slices of gold links---\linkerB{} contributes $137$ unique true positives, \linkerD{} another $15$, and \linkerC{} another $17$---so the decomposition is not redundant.

The table tells the RQ2 story from inside the approach.
Removing \linkerD{} or \linkerC{} barely moves the standard link-level \fone---$-0.4$pp and $-5.0$pp---but each one lowers the tail: with \linkerB{} alone, worst-component \fone\ falls from $0.71$ to $0.62$ and sentence coverage from $0.88$ to $0.74$.
Removing \linkerB{} is different---it collapses every metric, link-level \fone\ included, by $60.0$pp, because \linkerB{} recovers most of the direct links.
Neither of the two smaller linkers is usable alone: \linkerD{} reaches $0.078$ \avgfone\ and \linkerC{} $0.261$, and both drop at least one component to zero.

The knowledge module is ablated as the no-knowledge variant of \ref{rq:ablation}: the document-alias stage is switched off and every linker sees canonical component names only.
Removing the alias table costs $6.2$pp \avgfone\ ($0.914 \rightarrow 0.851$) and $9.6$pp \avgftwo\ ($0.931 \rightarrow 0.835$) on doc-model, a contribution no linker recovers on its own.
Its real weight is on the tail, exactly where \autoref{tab:rq2} says the standard metric is blind: worst-component \fone\ falls from $0.71$ to $0.37$ ($-33$pp) and the harmonic per-component \fone\ from $0.87$ to $0.60$ ($-27$pp), against $-9.6$pp of doc-code file \fone.
This is because an alias is the only route to a component the document never names canonically, and those components are small: losing them costs little link volume and a whole architectural unit.
Therefore the modules are complementary rather than interchangeable, and the knowledge module and the two cheap linkers all buy tail coverage that the standard metric does not price.

\begin{rqanswer}
\paragraph{Answer to RQ4.}
\linkerB{} alone beats SWATTR; \linkerD{} and \linkerC{} lift \avgfone\ from $0.856$ to $0.914$ and \avgftwo\ from $0.818$ to $0.931$; the knowledge module is worth $6.2$pp \avgfone\ and $9.6$pp \avgftwo.
Removing a small linker or the knowledge module barely moves link-level \fone\ but lowers the tail---the alias table alone is worth $33$pp of worst-component \fone---while removing \linkerB{} collapses every metric.
\end{rqanswer}

\subsection{Summary}
\label{sec:results:summary}

\approach{} improves over the strongest published baseline, \Artemis{}, at every granularity we measure.
Doc-model \avgfone\ rises from $0.836$ to $0.914$ ($+7.8$pp), and \avgftwo\ from $0.790$ to $0.931$ ($+14.1$pp), with four of five projects gaining \fone\ and all five gaining \ftwo.
The doc-code composition inherits the gain: $+6.0$pp \avgfone\ and $+10.8$pp \avgftwo.
Doc-model sentence coverage reaches $0.96$ ($+17$pp over both baselines) at a silent-failure mass of $2.2\%$, against $7.1\%$ and $7.4\%$.
The doc-code size-aware metrics show a much larger advantage on the tail that the standard metric hides: worst-component \fone\ $+36$pp, harmonic-mean per-component \fone\ $+41$pp, and sentence coverage $+17$pp over \Artemis{}.
The three judges remove $164.3$ distinct false positives at a cost of $14.0$ true links---worth $14.5$pp of \fone\ but only $4.9$pp of \ftwo, since a judge buys precision and never recall---and each of the three linkers catches a non-overlapping slice of true positives.
The knowledge module is worth $6.2$pp \avgfone, and $33$pp of worst-component \fone: like the size-aware suite itself, its value is concentrated in the tail.
We discuss the threats to validity in~\autoref{sec:threats}.
