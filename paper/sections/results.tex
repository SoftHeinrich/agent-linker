On doc-model, \approach{} covers $0.94$ of the documented sentences, against $0.79$ for both baselines ($+15$pp).

On doc-code, the standard link-level \fone\ shows the \emph{smallest} gap: \approach{} barely separates from \Artemis{} ($+5.7$pp), because it is driven by the few large components every system recovers.
The size-aware metrics open the gap.
\approach{} reaches a sentence coverage of $0.84$ against \Artemis{}'s $0.71$ ($+13$pp), because the judges reject links whose anchor is absent from the document while the linkers collectively visit every sentence at least once.

The difference is largest on the tail metrics.
\approach{} reaches a worst-component \fone\ of $0.75$ against \Artemis{}'s $0.35$ ($+41$pp, about seven times the link-level \fone\ gap).
\Artemis{} scores \fone\ $0$ on at least one gold component on two of five projects, BigBlueButton and JabRef, missing it entirely.
\approach{} never drops a component to zero; its lowest per-component \fone\ is $0.33$, on Teammates.
It keeps the tail stronger throughout.
The harmonic mean of the per-component \fone\ tells the same story without relying on a single component: $0.88$ for \approach{} against \Artemis{}'s $0.47$ ($+42$pp).
This is the failure mode that link-level \fone\ cannot show: a missed small component costs almost nothing in a volume-weighted average, but a developer who reads the architecture still sees the hole.

\approach{} has three modules: the knowledge module and the two linker modules \linkerB{} and \linkerC{}.
We ablate each one and score the result with the size-aware suite.
\autoref{tab:rq4} reports the four variants on doc-code, with the metrics ordered from the standard link-level \fone\ to the tail.

\linkerB{} alone reaches an \avgfone of $0.880$, already above SWATTR's $0.799$: an \ac{LLM} base beats the lexical pipeline on its own.
Adding \linkerC{} raises \avgfone to $0.936$, because \linkerC{} recovers the indirect references \linkerB{} cannot quote.
The two linkers catch different slices of gold links -- \linkerB{} contributes $137$ unique true positives and \linkerC{} another $20$ -- so the decomposition is not redundant.

The table tells the RQ2 story from inside the approach.
Removing \linkerC{} or the knowledge module barely moves the standard link-level \fone, but it lowers the tail metrics: \linkerC{} removal trims the worst-component and harmonic scores, and the no-knowledge variant drops the tail sharply.
Removing \linkerB{} is different---it collapses every metric, link-level \fone\ included, because \linkerB{} recovers most of the direct links.

The knowledge module is ablated as the no-knowledge variant of \ref{rq:ablation}: removing the alias table and ambiguity map that both linkers read from costs $5.8$pp \avgfone, a contribution neither linker can recover on its own.

\begin{rqanswer}
\paragraph{Answer to RQ4.}
\linkerB{} alone beats SWATTR; \linkerC{} lifts \avgfone from $0.880$ to $0.936$; the knowledge module adds $5.8$pp.
Removing \linkerC{} or knowledge barely moves link-level \fone\ but lowers the tail metrics; removing \linkerB{} collapses every metric.
\end{rqanswer}

\subsection{Summary}
\label{sec:results:summary}

\approach{} improves over the strongest published baseline, \Artemis{}, at every granularity we measure.
Doc-model \avgfone rises from $0.836$ to $0.936$ ($+10.0$pp), with all five projects gaining.
The doc-code composition inherits the gain, a $+5.7$pp \avgfone.
Doc-model sentence coverage reaches $0.94$ ($+15$pp over both baselines) with $0.0\%$ silent-failure mass.
The doc-code size-aware metrics show a much larger advantage on the tail that the standard metric hides: worst-component \fone\ $+41$pp, harmonic-mean per-component \fone\ $+42$pp, and sentence coverage $+13$pp over \Artemis{}.
The two judges remove false positives roughly additively while attacking separate failure modes.
Each of the two linkers catches a non-overlapping slice of true positives.
We discuss the threats to validity in~\autoref{sec:threats}.
