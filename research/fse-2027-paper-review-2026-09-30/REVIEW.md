## What the paper contributes, and what the evidence supports

The primary question is whether a training-free LLM workflow can recover
architecture-document-to-component links more effectively on the selected
benchmark. A shared alias stage feeds two routes; accepted links are composed
with a fixed component-to-code mapping for the downstream task. A second
contribution is a diagnostic suite that exposes variation among components.

The strongest parts are the matched-backend baseline comparison, the published
per-project results (including losses), the preservation of run-level artifacts,
and the explicit separation of candidate generation from filtering. The metric
motivation correctly points out that aggregate scores can conceal group-level
failures. These strengths survive the problems below; the review does not treat
a reporting error as proof that all experimental results are invalid.

For s126 and Artemis on **GPT-5.6-terra, five projects, three runs each**, the
current saved-link macro averages reproduce these differences:

## Argument map

1. Vocabulary differences and contextual references complicate linking.
   **Supported as a task motivation; the claim that all baselines mostly use
   word overlap is not supported (12).**
2. Shared aliases, separate routes, and evidence-informed filtering address
   these problems. **A plausible design; important actual filters and judge
   inputs differ from the description (7, 8, 24).**
3. Higher recorded benchmark scores and ablations demonstrate a contribution.
   **The full-system numerical comparison reproduces, but the judge treatment
   and mechanism attribution need correction (3, 10, 25, 27).**
4. Unequal component link counts make aggregate scores incomplete diagnostics.
   **Supported, but the Gini illustration and some denominators are wrong
   (14–18).**
5. Larger gaps under new component metrics establish architectural usefulness.
   **This transition remains a hypothesis; the metrics encode preferences that
   are not independently validated (29, 30).**

### FSE-16 — The component metrics change the evaluated domain as well as the weights

**Severity:** High · **Status:** Verified · **FSE:** Soundness / metric specification

**Location:** PDF p.9; [paper/sections/metric.tex:48](/mnt/hostshare/ardoco-home/agent-linker/paper/sections/metric.tex:48). Anchor: “Let”.

**Problem and consequence:** The component evaluator uses gold SAM→code ownership, drops Interface elements, can count a shared file in several components, and excludes predictions outside the gold-reachable component universe.
