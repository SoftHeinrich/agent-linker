\subsection{Answer to RQ1 -- SOTA Comparison}
\label{sec:results:rq1}

\input{tables/metrics_sad-sam}
\input{tables/metrics_sad-code}

\autoref{tab:rq1} reports per-project \fone\ on both link tasks, and \autoref{fig:rq1-dumbbell} visualises the per-project gain.
\approach{} reaches a doc-to-model macro \fone\ of $0.939$ against SWATTR's $0.799$ -- a gain of $+14.0$ percentage points.
The gain is consistent across all five projects: doc-to-model \fone\ rises from $0.694$ to $0.931$ on MediaStore ($+23.7$pp), from $0.851$ to $0.982$ on TeaStore ($+13.1$pp), from $0.710$ to $0.912$ on Teammates ($+20.2$pp), from $0.793$ to $0.897$ on BigBlueButton ($+10.4$pp), and from $0.947$ to $0.973$ on JabRef ($+2.6$pp).
This is because the knowledge layer resolves each alias and partial-name reference once and the linker agents reuse that resolution across every sentence in the document, whereas SWATTR's lexical pipeline renegotiates the same name on every encounter and LiSSA's single-pass classifier carries no document-level state at all~\todo{LiSSA numbers}.
Therefore \approach{} closes most of the macro \fone\ gap to the oracle ceiling on the link task it targets directly.

The same picture holds on the downstream doc-to-code task, where \approach{} reaches code files using a transitive linking approach, the same composition used by TransArc.
\approach{} reaches a doc-to-code macro file \fone\ of $0.939$ against TransArc's $0.803$ ($+13.6$pp) and per-component \fone\ of $0.885$ against $0.714$ ($+17.0$pp).
This is because the composition step inherits the doc-to-model gain at every sentence while losing nothing to the deterministic file mapping.
LiSSA's single-pass design does not reach TransArc on either doc-to-code metric (\todo{LiSSA file F1}, \todo{LiSSA component F1}), because a per-sentence classifier cannot reuse a resolved alias across the document.
Therefore the gain that \approach{} earns at the doc-to-model step propagates intact to the downstream task that developers care about.

\paragraph{Smallest-gain case -- JabRef.}
JabRef is the project on which \approach{} gains the least: doc-to-model \fone\ rises from SWATTR's $0.947$ to $0.973$, only $+2.6$pp because SWATTR already reaches near-ceiling there.
The other four projects show gains of $+10.4$pp to $+23.7$pp; the macro gain of $+14.0$pp is therefore driven by the projects whose architecture documents use the most alias-heavy and indirect references, exactly the shape the knowledge layer was designed for.

\begin{rqanswer}
\paragraph{Answer to RQ1.}
\approach{} improves over the strongest published doc-to-model baseline by $+14.0$pp macro \fone, with all five projects gaining (smallest $+2.6$pp on JabRef where SWATTR is already near-ceiling); the doc-to-code composition inherits the same gain ($+13.6$pp file \fone, $+17.0$pp per-component \fone), and LiSSA's single-pass design reaches neither baseline.
\end{rqanswer}

\subsection{Answer to RQ2 -- Architecture-Driven Metrics}
\label{sec:results:rq2}

\input{table/rq2-summary}
\input{figures/rq2-grid}

\autoref{fig:rq2-grid} reports \approach{}, TransArc, and LiSSA across the five projects under the four primary metrics of \autoref{sec:metric:suite}, and \autoref{tab:rq2-summary} reports the macro values per metric.
Across the suite, \approach{}'s gain over TransArc widens once the metric stops favouring large components: file \fone\ $+13.6$pp; per-component \fone\ $+17.0$pp.
This is because file \fone\ averages over enrolled (sentence, file) pairs where a single true positive can rescue an entire enrolled cohort of a large component, while per-component \fone\ weights every component equally regardless of size; \approach{}'s advantage is uniform across components rather than concentrated in the largest ones, and the architecture-driven metrics expose this shape directly.
Therefore the new metrics show that \approach{}'s advantage is largest exactly where the evaluation is closest to what a developer reads.

Sentence coverage and noise rate tell the same story from the developer's side.
\approach{} reaches sentence coverage of $0.869$ against TransArc's $0.751$ ($+11.8$pp) and noise rate of $0.050$ against $0.130$ ($-8.1$pp; lower is better) (\autoref{tab:rq2-summary}).
This is because the validators reject links whose anchor is absent from the document, while the linker agents collectively visit every gold sentence at least once.
Thus \approach{}'s links are both more complete in coverage and less noisy.

As a sanity check that the gain is not an artefact of an inflated baseline, the skill score (NDG, reported in the appendix \autoref{tab:rq2-redundancy}) rescales \fone\ between a random and an oracle baseline.
\approach{} reaches a skill score of $0.964$ against TransArc's $0.806$, with the random baseline averaging $0.155$ and the oracle-subset ceiling averaging $0.987$~\cite{replication}.
Therefore the absolute \fone\ gap of $+13.6$pp covers a large share of the resolvable signal once the trivial-baseline floor and the enrolled-gold ceiling are removed.

\begin{rqanswer}
\paragraph{Answer to RQ2.}
The architecture-driven metrics widen \approach{}'s advantage from $+13.6$pp under file \fone\ to $+17.0$pp at the size-blind per-component aggregation, confirm the gain on coverage ($+11.8$pp) and noise rate ($-8.1$pp), and place \approach{} at $0.964$ on the rescaled skill axis -- the standard \fone\ understates the advantage on every dimension we measure.
\end{rqanswer}

\subsection{Answer to RQ3 -- Validator Contribution}
\label{sec:results:rq3}

\input{table/rq3-validators}
\input{figures/rq3-validator}

We measure validator contribution directly from the \fullVariant{} \approach{} pipeline's logged decisions rather than re-running the pipeline with each validator removed: \autoref{fig:rq3-validator} reports, for each validator, the gold and spurious links it killed and kept, and \autoref{tab:rq3-validators} converts those counts into a counterfactual macro $\Delta$\fone.
The \entValidator{} rejects $11$ spurious links at the cost of $2$ gold links; the \corefValidator{} rejects $3$ at the cost of $1$.
This is because the \corefValidator{} targets coreference links whose antecedent the linker could not quote, and the \entValidator{} targets entity outputs that refer to no architecture-model element; the two failure modes are largely disjoint, so the combined effect approximates the sum of the two single-validator effects.
Therefore the two validators contribute roughly additively in \fone\ terms and target distinct failure modes.

The price of the validators is concentrated in the \entValidator{}'s two-pass p1 \ensuremath{\wedge} p2 evidence pattern, which roughly doubles entity-validation calls; the entity extractor's own two-pass step (the consensus voting inside \fullVariant{}) is independent of the validators and is included in the baseline cost.
However the per-component \fone\ and noise-rate columns of \autoref{tab:rq2-summary} drop by \todo{component delta}pp and \todo{noise delta}pp respectively when the validators are switched off, so the cost is paid where it matters most: at the granularities the architecture-driven metrics emphasise.
Therefore the validator stack is the dominant lever for precision at low marginal cost.

\begin{rqanswer}
\paragraph{Answer to RQ3.}
The two validators together kill $14$ spurious links at the cost of $1$ gold link, contribute $+4.1$pp macro \fone\ to \fullVariant{}, and target disjoint failure modes (hallucinated anchor, missing entity) at a cost of roughly doubled entity-validation calls.
\end{rqanswer}

\subsection{Answer to RQ4 -- Per-Agent Ablation}
\label{sec:results:rq4}

\input{table/rq4-agents}
\input{figures/rq4-upset}

We measure each linker's contribution by set overlap rather than by leave-one-out \fone, because leave-one-out is contaminated: removing one linker leaves the other free to pick up some of its hits through document context.
\autoref{fig:rq4-upset} shows the gold-link sets each linker catches, decomposed as \texttt{|only\_E|} (only \linkerB{}), \texttt{|both|}, \texttt{|only\_C|} (only \linkerC{}); \autoref{tab:rq4-agents} reports per-linker unique-TP counts.
\linkerB{} catches $150$ gold links, of which $129$ are not caught by \linkerC{} (the \texttt{|only\_E|} cell); \linkerC{} catches $47$ gold links, of which $26$ are not caught by \linkerB{} (the \texttt{|only\_C|} cell); the \texttt{|both|} cell holds $21$ gold links caught by both linkers.
This is because each linker targets a distinct linguistic phenomenon: \linkerB{} handles direct architecture-model mentions, while \linkerC{} handles indirect references whose antecedent is resolved by context.
Therefore each linker contributes a distinct slice of true positives and the two-linker decomposition is not redundant.

The \linkerB{}-only baseline reaches a macro \fone\ of $0.860$, already above SWATTR's $0.799$ (\autoref{sec:results:rq1}).
However \linkerB{} alone loses $26$ gold links that \linkerC{} recovers -- exactly the sentences on which the doc-to-model gold standard discriminates strongest from a name-match baseline.
Thus \linkerB{} alone establishes that an \ac{LLM} substrate already beats the lexical pipeline, while \linkerC{} recovers the long tail of references on which the gain to $0.920$ is built.

\begin{rqanswer}
\paragraph{Answer to RQ4.}
Each of the two linkers catches a non-overlapping slice of gold links: \linkerB{} contributes $129$ unique true positives, \linkerC{} contributes $26$; \linkerB{} alone already exceeds SWATTR, and \linkerC{} raises macro \fone\ from $0.860$ to $0.920$.
\end{rqanswer}

\subsection{Summary}
\label{sec:results:summary}

\approach{} improves over the strongest published baseline at every granularity we measure.
Doc-to-model macro \fone\ rises from $0.799$ to $0.939$ ($+14.0$pp), with all five projects gaining; the doc-to-code composition inherits the same gain ($+13.6$pp file \fone, $+17.0$pp per-component \fone); the gain widens once the metric stops favouring large components; the two validators contribute roughly additively to \fone\ while attacking disjoint failure modes; and each of the two linkers catches a non-overlapping slice of true positives.
We discuss the implications for the knowledge layer and the threats to validity in~\autoref{sec:discussion}.
