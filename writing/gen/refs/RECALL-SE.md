## Pattern from memory

From the entries above, the dominant body order for SE papers that contribute BOTH a method/system AND an evaluation metric/methodology appears to be **metric/methodology-first when the critique of existing evaluation IS the headline contribution**, and **method-first when the metric is secondary scaffolding for evaluating the technique**.

Evidence:
- **Metric-first**: CrystalBLEU (1), CodeBLEU (2), EvalPlus (5), Pearson et al. SBFL (8), Shi/Roy on code-summarisation BLEU (10), Just et al. mutants-vs-real-faults (12). In each, the paper opens by problematising the existing metric, then introduces the new measure, then validates it (sometimes with a small accompanying technique).
- **Method-first**: GumTree (11), MUSE (7), Defects4J (3 — actually interleaved). Here the algorithm/dataset is the headline; the metric is introduced as needed in the evaluation section.

**Net pattern**: when a paper's primary self-positioning is "prior evaluation is wrong", body order is metric-first. When the self-positioning is "here is a new technique that happens to need a custom metric to fairly measure it", body order is method-first. Mixed/interleaved is common for benchmark papers (Defects4J, SWE-bench) where dataset construction and evaluation protocol are inseparable.

For a paper claiming both contributions equally, **metric-first is the slightly safer default in modern (2020+) SE venues** because reviewers respond well to evaluation-critique framing. Method-first remains common at ICSE/ASE where novelty of the artifact is weighted heavily.
